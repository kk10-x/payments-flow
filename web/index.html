A gateway, three card providers, one ledger

Follow a payment through the gateway.

Each dot is a payment passing through a gateway that routes it to one of three card providers. Captured payments reach the ledger on the right. The ones that don't are marked with a cross, and the tape below the diagram says why.

Routing

Route

The gateway scores each provider by its recent success rate and latency, then sends each payment to a provider chosen at random, weighted by that score. A healthy provider gets most of the traffic, and a longer bar means a healthier one.

Circuit breaker

Degrade

Beacon is now declining 90% of charges. Its bar shrinks, traffic drains toward Atlas and Crest, and after four failures in a row its circuit breaker opens. The box is hatched and gets no traffic for three seconds, then one probe payment tests it.

Retry policy

Retry

Atlas now charges the card, then loses 40% of its replies. The gateway can't tell whether that payment went through. So it retries Atlas with the same idempotency key, and Atlas returns the charge it already made. Sending it to Beacon instead could charge the card twice.

Declines, by contrast, charged nothing, so those do fail over.

Idempotency

Replay

A client that retries a request with the same idempotency key gets the original result, never a second charge. The hollow dots bounce straight back from the gateway. Send one payment 25 times at once and count the charges.

 

Or send one yourself

Send the same key twice: you get the same payment back and the replay count goes up.

    Routing

    Each provider gets a score: its recent success rate squared, divided by one plus its latency over 300 ms. The gateway picks a provider at random with weight score cubed, so a healthy provider gets most of the traffic. A small floor keeps a struggling provider sampled, so its numbers stay fresh and it can recover.

    Circuit breaker

    Closed is normal. After four failures in a row the breaker is open: no traffic for three seconds. Then it goes probe: exactly one payment is let through. If that succeeds the breaker closes; if it fails, it opens again.

    Not modelled

    Real providers or card data. Durable storage for idempotency keys (they live in memory). A reconciliation job for unknown payments. Currencies other than INR. Amounts are shown in rupees, with L for lakh and Cr for crore.
